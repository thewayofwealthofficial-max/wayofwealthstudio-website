**What you need to know**
- Charging for your healing work doesn't make it less sacred. It makes it sustainable.
- The guilt you feel when you name a price is a learned response, not a moral signal.
- What happens to the money *after* it arrives matters just as much as getting comfortable receiving it.

---

If you've ever typed a price into a message, deleted it, typed it again, and then knocked it down before hitting send, this is for you.

You got into this work because something in you knew it was real. You felt it. You wanted to share it. Money was never the point. And now here you are, with a real practice, real results, real clients who are genuinely helped, and you still flinch every time someone asks what you charge.

That flinch isn't weakness. It's information. And this post is going to tell you what it's actually about.

The quick answer: you charge because your time, your training, and your presence are real resources. Giving them away until you have nothing left doesn't serve anyone.

---

## What you might be telling yourself

**"I just want to help people."**
You do. And a depleted, burned-out version of you helps fewer people. Charging keeps you in the game.

**"Deep down I know what I'm worth, but I feel guilty charging it."**
That gap between knowing and doing is the whole problem. Knowing isn't enough. The guilt is doing the overriding.

**"I've done all the courses and nothing landed."**
That's because the block isn't information. You already have the information. It's the feeling underneath that hasn't shifted yet.

**"I refuse to charge, and the universe always provides."**
Sometimes that works. Often, as a lot of people eventually admit, it ends in burnout. The universe providing and you getting paid aren't opposites.

**"I just have to feel better about myself before I can change my prices."**
This one keeps you waiting forever. The feeling usually follows the action, not the other way around.

**"I'm so terrified someone will actually book a call."**
So you hold back on sharing, stay quiet, and then wonder why months pass without movement. The terror is real. Waiting for it to go away first isn't the strategy.

## Why this hits harder in healing work

Most professions have a clear separation between the service and the person delivering it. A solicitor charges for legal advice. Nobody questions whether the law should be free.

Healing work is different. You are the instrument. The energy, the presence, the holding of space, it all flows through you. So when someone questions your price, it can feel like they're questioning your gift, or your worthiness to have it.

Your training probably didn't help with this either. In a lot of healing and spiritual traditions, the culture around money quietly teaches that sacred work and payment don't belong together. You absorbed that. It sits right alongside your bank balance and creates a kind of internal conflict that no pricing spreadsheet is going to fix.

And because so much of your community shares the same belief, undercharging looks like virtue. It gets reinforced. The practitioner who charges properly can look greedy. The one who gives it all away looks devoted. That's a powerful social pressure.

## A scene you'll recognise

You finish a session. The client is visibly moved. They say it was the most profound thing they've ever experienced. You feel it too. Then comes the moment you have to say the number. Your voice changes. You add "but I can do a discount" before they've even blinked.

They would have paid full price. You just decided for them that they wouldn't.

## What happens once the money actually arrives

Here's the part most posts skip. You work up the courage to charge properly. A larger payment lands. And then, somehow, it's gone within a few weeks. You're not sure where.

Several people in communities like yours have said exactly this: big month, big spend, and then the same low-level scarcity feeling returns. It can feel like your body doesn't know how to hold a larger amount. Like it needs to be moved on quickly.

That pattern is worth paying attention to. The work on charging is only half of it. [Why do I splurge in a good month and panic in a bad one?](/blog/why-do-i-splurge-in-a-good-month-and-panic-in-a-bad-one/) goes into this side of it in more detail.

## What's actually going on in your head

A few real behavioural ideas explain what you're experiencing.

Brad and Ted Klontz, financial psychologists who developed the concept of **money scripts**, found that we carry deep, largely unconscious beliefs about money, picked up early in life, that drive financial behaviour in ways we don't fully see. A belief like "money is spiritual corruption" or "charging for sacred work is wrong" is a money script. It runs in the background. Knowing it's there is the first step to working with it.

Then there's **cognitive dissonance**. You hold two beliefs at once: "I deserve to be paid fairly" and "charging full price feels wrong." The mind hates holding both, so it resolves the tension by dropping one. Usually the one that costs you money. You discount, you undercharge, you give it away, because that removes the discomfort. It's a mental shortcut, not a moral choice.

And if you've had a big payment land and watched it disappear, that can partly be explained by Richard Thaler's work on **mental accounting**. We don't treat all money the same. Income that arrives irregularly, in a lump, can feel different to "regular" money. Less real, somehow. More available for spending. So it gets spent.

## One thing to try today

Before your next session or before you next quote a price, write the number down on paper first. Not in a message, not in an email draft. On paper. Say it out loud to yourself, in a room alone.

You're not performing confidence. You're just letting your own number stop being a shock to your own system. That's it. One step.

## Questions people ask

### Is it spiritually wrong to charge for energy healing?

Different traditions have different views, and this post isn't going to tell you what yours should be. What's worth noticing is that the belief "it's wrong to charge" often produces burnout, not generosity. A sustainable practice serves more people over more years.

### Why do I always undercharge even when I know I shouldn't?

Knowing the right number and being able to say it are two different things. Klontz's work on money scripts suggests there's often a deeper, older belief running underneath, one that hasn't been examined yet. The behaviour follows the belief, not the spreadsheet.

### Why do I feel guilty after I get paid well?

That guilt can be a sign that part of you hasn't accepted that the exchange was fair. It can also show up as overspending immediately after a big payment, which is a way of resolving the discomfort quickly. Noticing the pattern is more useful than judging it.

### How do I stop discounting before the client even asks?

Discounting before someone objects is a preemptive way of avoiding rejection. It's not generosity. Writing your price down and saying it out loud before conversations is a small practice that starts to make the number feel more solid in your own body.

### Can I build a real income from healing work without compromising the work itself?

Yes. The practitioners who stay in this work for decades are usually the ones who worked out how to make it financially viable. Sustainability and integrity aren't opposites. They depend on each other.
