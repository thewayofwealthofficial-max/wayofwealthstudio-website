**What you need to know**
- Some healers do offer free work, but most who do it without a clear boundary burn out and stop altogether.
- The guilt you feel when you say your price is a behaviour pattern, not a character flaw.
- What happens to money once it arrives matters just as much as charging for it.

---

If you are an energy healer asking this question, you are probably not just curious about what other practitioners do. You are trying to figure out if it is okay to charge at all. Or you are exhausted from giving your work away and looking for permission to stop. Either way, you are in the right place.

There is a pull that almost every wellness practitioner feels. It goes something like: this work is sacred, and sacred things should not have a price tag. You got into this because you wanted to help. And somewhere along the way, helping and charging started to feel like they were on opposite sides.

This post is going to name what is actually happening, give you the behaviour science behind it, and hand you one small thing you can try today.

The quick answer is this: yes, some energy healers offer free sessions, usually as tasters or as part of a community exchange, but very few sustain a free-only model without burning out or stopping the work entirely.

---

## What you might be telling yourself

**"I just want to help people."**
Wanting to help is real and good. But it does not pay rent, and when it runs out, the helping stops.

**"I know what I'm worth deep down, I just feel guilty charging it."**
That gap between knowing and feeling is where almost all of this lives. It is not about knowing more.

**"I refuse to charge. The universe will compensate me."**
Some people genuinely experience this. But what often happens instead is slow financial pressure, then resentment, then burnout. The belief is not the problem. The behaviour has consequences either way.

**"I just need to feel better about myself before I raise my prices."**
Waiting to feel ready first usually means waiting forever. The feeling tends to come after the action, not before.

**"I've done all the courses and nothing has landed."**
Because the gap is not information. You already know what to charge. The block lives somewhere else.

**"I'm so terrified someone will actually book a call."**
That terror is data. It is telling you something about what you believe will happen when they say yes.

## Why this hits healing work harder than almost anything else

Your training probably did not include a single hour on pricing, sales or what to do when a big payment lands. It was all about the work itself. And the culture around healing, breathwork, yoga, meditation, somatic practice, has a story running through it: that this kind of care should flow freely, like water.

That story has roots. And it creates a specific kind of pressure that, say, a software developer just does not feel when they send an invoice.

There is also the fact that your work lives in your body. You are the product. So when someone pushes back on your price, it can feel like they are pushing back on you. Not on a service. On your worth as a person.

## A scene you might recognise

Someone messages you. They say they cannot really afford your rate but they really need help. You say yes anyway. The session goes well. They are grateful. You feel good for about an hour.

Then you notice you are a bit tired. A bit resentful. You are not sure of who, exactly. You still do not feel like you can raise your prices, because look, you are already helping someone who cannot afford it. What kind of person charges more than that?

That loop can run for years.

## What happens to the money once it actually arrives

Here is the part most people do not see, and I want to say it gently because it comes up a lot.

Many wellness practitioners say spending is not their problem. Charging is the problem. And then when a bigger payment arrives, something shifts. It can feel like your body does not know how to hold it. There is a kind of internal alarm that says this is too much, this is not safe, this does not belong to you.

So it leaves. Fast. Overspending on things that feel like relief, or just quietly draining away into expenses that were always there but suddenly feel fine to ignore.

The charging and the spending are connected to the same identity story. If you believe, somewhere underneath, that you do not quite deserve the money, it will find a way out just as fast as it came in.

## The behaviour behind it

Dan Ariely's work on social norms and market norms is one of the clearest explanations I know. When we do something in the social norm world, like helping a friend move, or sharing healing with someone we love, putting a price on it feels wrong. It breaks the feeling of the exchange.

Healing work sits right on that boundary. It feels like love. It feels like care. And love does not send an invoice. So the moment you put a price on it, your brain fires a signal that says: you are doing something wrong.

But here is the thing. You are not in the social norm world. You built a business. You have clients, not friends. The exchange changed, even if the feeling did not catch up.

Brad Klontz's research on money scripts is useful here too. A money script is a belief about money, usually formed early, that runs automatically. Things like "money is not spiritual" or "wanting money is selfish" or "good people give freely." These scripts are not conscious choices. They just run. And they can drive your pricing decisions without you ever noticing.

The identity piece matters as well. Your price carries meaning about who you are. When your whole sense of self is tied to being a healer who gives, a higher number can feel like a betrayal of that. That is what makes it so hard to change, and why knowing the right price is never quite enough on its own.

## One small step to try today

Write down two numbers. The number you charge right now. The number you would charge if you were not afraid of anyone's reaction.

Just write them. Do not send anything. Do not post anything. Do not change anything yet.

Then sit with the gap between them and ask: what do I think will happen if I charge the second number? Write that down too. Not to fix it today. Just to see it clearly, because you cannot work with something you cannot see.

## Questions people ask

### Do energy healers have to charge for their services?

No, there is no rule that says you must. But working entirely for free is a model that very few people sustain without burning out or eventually stopping the work. What matters is that your model is chosen, not defaulted into out of fear.

### Is it spiritually wrong to charge for healing?

Different traditions have different views, and this post is not going to settle that. What behavioural research does show, through Ariely's work on social and market norms, is that the discomfort you feel around charging is a common psychological response, not a sign you are doing something wrong.

### Why do I feel guilty every time I give my price?

That guilt is usually a money script running in the background, a belief formed long before you started your practice. Klontz's research on money scripts shows these beliefs operate automatically and shape financial decisions without feeling like beliefs at all. Naming yours is the first move.

### Can offering free sessions help grow my healing business?

Free sessions used intentionally, as a taster with a clear structure and a next step, can work. Free sessions offered out of guilt or fear of charging tend to attract clients who do not convert, and they deplete you faster than almost anything else in business.

### Why do I earn well some months and barely anything the next?

Feast and famine income is extremely common in wellness businesses and often has less to do with your audience and more to do with inconsistent offers, inconsistent visibility, or unconscious patterns around receiving.
