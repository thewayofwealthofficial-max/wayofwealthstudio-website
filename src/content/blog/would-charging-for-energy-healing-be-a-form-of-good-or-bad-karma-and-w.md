**What you need to know**
- Charging for your work does not cancel out the care behind it.
- The guilt you feel when you say a price is a learned pattern, and patterns can shift.
- What happens to the money after it arrives matters just as much as how you receive it.

If you do energy healing, breathwork, or any practice where the gift feels like it came through you rather than from you, the question of charging probably sits heavy. You wonder whether taking money changes the purity of what you offer. You wonder whether the universe will keep sending clients if you put a number on it.

That question is real. It deserves a straight answer.

This post gives you one. It also shows you what tends to be happening underneath the question, because the karma angle is usually the surface and there is something quieter running below it.

---

**The quick answer:** Charging for energy healing does not create bad karma. Burning out and closing your practice because you gave everything away does.

---

## What you might be telling yourself

**"I just want to help people."**
Wanting to help is true and good. But helping from an empty account stops being help pretty quickly.

**"Deep down I know what I'm worth, but saying the price out loud feels wrong."**
That gap between knowing and saying is where most of the work lives. The knowing is there. The feeling hasn't caught up yet.

**"I refuse to charge. The universe always compensates me."**
Sometimes it does. But one practitioner said exactly this, gave freely for years, and ended up completely burned out. The universe does not pay your rent on a schedule you can plan around.

**"I've done all the courses and nothing has actually landed."**
That's because the block rarely lives in information. You already know the concepts. The pattern is older than the courses.

**"I have to feel better about myself before I can raise my prices."**
This one keeps the price low indefinitely, because the feeling of being ready tends to come after the action, not before it.

**"I charge, but nobody is buying."**
That's a different problem from guilt, and worth separating out. Pricing and visibility are two separate levers. Pulling the wrong one first keeps you stuck.

## Why this hits your kind of work harder

Most professions don't have a spiritual framework built into the job description. A plumber does not wonder whether fixing pipes is a gift from source. You do.

Your training probably included something about service, about holding space, about not letting ego or money corrupt the work. That teaching is often genuinely useful. But it can leave a residue: a felt sense that charging is the opposite of serving.

Add to that the culture in healing communities, where swapping, gifting, and paying what you can has often been the norm. Pricing at full rate can feel like breaking a social agreement, even when no one spelled that agreement out.

And then there is the plain fact that your work is invisible. A massage therapist has hands on a body. You are often working with something no one can see or measure. That makes it harder to point at a thing and say: this is what you are paying for.

None of that makes the guilt rational. But it makes it very understandable.

## A scene you'll recognise

Someone messages to say the session changed their life. You feel warm, full, certain you are in the right work. Then they ask your rate for a follow-up. You type a number, delete it, type something lower, feel your chest tighten, hit send, and immediately wonder if it was still too much.

The work felt sacred. The transaction felt grubby. So you tell yourself the problem is the money.

The problem is the feeling, and that feeling has a history.

---

## What happens after the money arrives

Here's the part most posts skip. A lot of practitioners who struggle to charge also struggle, quietly, with what happens when a big payment does land. It can feel like your body doesn't quite know how to hold it. There's a relief, then a looseness, then somehow it's gone.

One person put it plainly: "I have no problem spending money, but charging for my services? That's where I freeze." Another said that getting a large payment triggered more anxiety, not less, because now there was more to lose.

This is worth paying attention to. If receiving feels uncomfortable, spending can become the fastest way to get back to familiar ground. The money leaves and the discomfort leaves with it.

That cycle, earn, feel uneasy, spend, repeat, keeps the financial floor low no matter what you charge.

---

## The idea that explains a lot of this

Brad Klontz, a psychologist who researches financial behaviour, talks about money scripts: the unconscious beliefs we formed early, usually before we had words for them. Things like "money is spiritual corruption" or "good people don't need much." These scripts run quietly in the background and shape every pricing conversation you have.

The relevant one here is the belief that charging and caring are opposites. Once you can see it as a belief rather than a truth, you have somewhere to stand.

You can read more about what drives the fear underneath all of this in [Why do I feel guilty charging what I'm actually worth?](/blog/why-do-i-feel-guilty-charging-what-im-actually-worth/)

## One thing to try today

Before your next session, write down the number you actually want to charge. Not the number you will send. The one that feels right when no one is watching. Just write it somewhere private.

You do not have to send it. You are just practising the act of naming it without immediately shrinking it. That gap between the real number and the sent number is information. It shows you exactly where the pattern lives.

## Questions people ask

### Does charging for spiritual work affect the energy or integrity of the session?

The intention you bring to the work and the fee you charge are separate things. Many practitioners find that charging a fair rate actually raises their commitment and focus, because they are not quietly resenting the exchange.

### Is it bad karma to put a price on healing?

Karma, across most frameworks that use the word, relates to intention and action together. The intention to serve combined with an action that sustains your ability to serve is hard to frame as harm. What does cause harm is giving past the point of sustainability and then disappearing from the work entirely.

### Why do I feel guilty when someone pays me for energy work?

Guilt at the point of payment is usually a money script running in the background, a belief formed earlier in life that says money and spiritual service don't belong together. Noticing the belief is the first step. A financial therapist or psychologist who works with money behaviour can help you go further.

### What should I charge for energy healing?

That's a business and personal decision that depends on your market, your costs, and your capacity, none of which I can know from here. What I can say is that undercharging until burnout helps no client. A business mentor who works with wellness practitioners can help you think through your specific situation.

### Why do I overspend after a good month in my practice?

Spending after a big payment can be a way of releasing the discomfort that comes with having more than feels normal. It gets the number back to familiar territory. [Why do I splurge in a good month and panic in a bad one?](/blog/why-do-i-splurge-in-a-good-month-and-panic-in-a-bad-one/) goes into this in more detail.
