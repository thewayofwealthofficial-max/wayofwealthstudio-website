robots). `_keep/VOICE_PROFILE.md` said its own gap out loud: "his camera voice is not sampled". This fills it.*

**Built from two measured sources, nothing described from memory:**
- **Calls:** 32 coaching calls, 166,626 words of Joel talking (`_keep/LINGUISTIC_PROFILE.md`, `JOEL_PHRASEBOOK.md`).
- **Camera:** a 2 hour 31 minute solo money psychology masterclass to camera, 22 Sep 2026, 23,034 words

**For posts, reels, emails and blogs, the camera voice is the model.** It's Joel talking to one person
who isn't talking back, which is what all content is. The call voice is the same man, looser.

Giving a "let's say" example (camera, verbatim):

Asking himself the question, then answering (camera, verbatim):

A run of questions to the reader (camera, verbatim):

## 9. Voice patterns

**Rhythm, measured on camera:**
- **Sentences:** median **11 words**, mean 13.8. 15% under five words, **21% over twenty.** Reading grade 6.3.
  (Calls: median 7, 37% under five. On camera he's steadier and longer.)
- **Pace:** 152 words a minute overall. A short pause every **10 words**. A one-second-plus pause every
  **40 words**. A two-second-plus pause every **163 words**.
- **Breath groups** (words between pauses): median **7**, mean 10, longest 1 in 10 runs to 23.

**What that means for writing him:**
- **A line is about 7 to 10 words.** That's one breath. Break lines there in captions and scripts.
- **A paragraph is about 40 words**, where he takes a real pause. Then he restarts with **"So", "And" or "Now"**
  (after a 1s+ pause: "So" 101 times, "And" 70, "Now" 37).
- **A new section comes about every 160 words**, after a long pause.
- **He rolls, then lands.** A long sentence joined with *and / because / so* carries the reasoning, a short one
  lands it, then he checks in: *"right?"* or *"okay?"*.

**Recurring moves:**
1. **Asks the reader's question out loud, then answers it.** "Now, why does this matter? Well, because…" ·
   "What is a money script? So a money script is…" 11% of his sentences are questions.
2. **"Let's say…" scenarios** from ordinary life: a night out, a parking ticket, a credit card. 26 times.
3. **Names the idea, then says it plainly in the next breath, restarting till it's plain.** "Loss aversion
   basically means you are going to experience losing... No, I'll say it this way. Loss aversion is expe-- you're
   gonna feel the, the pain, the pain of losing something is gonna feel twice as strong as the pain of
   gain-gaining something." (In writing say "about twice": `TEACHING_SCOPE.md`.)
4. **"We", not "you should".** *"We file money into mental pots."* · *"We harness the power of mental accounting."*
5. **Signposts where he's going.** "I'm gonna…", "we're gonna…", "I'll get into that in a second", "moving on".
6. **A run of short questions** that walks the reader through their own past.
7. **Self-corrects in the open** ("No, I'll say it this way.") and owns a ramble.

**Camera vs calls, the difference that matters:** on camera "like" drops from 273 to 43 per 10,000 words,
"you know" from 102 to 17, and dude, bro and mate disappear. **So written content gets a light touch of "like"
and "you know", not the call level.** "So", "now", "right?", "let's say" and "very / really / actually" stay.

**Never copy from the camera talk:** a family member's health · the name of the firm he worked for · the
"forty-eight percent more likely" savings figure (not in `TEACHING_SCOPE.md`) · the 60/20/20 and 60/40 splits
(close to advice) · lines aimed at "holistic healers" (old audience).

## 10. Email notes

Sign-off **"Joel"** on its own line. **British spelling** (his camera talk is transcribed in American spelling;
write British). No em dashes. Contractions always ("you're", "it's", "don't"). "Gonna" is his spoken form; in
writing use "going to" unless it's a script he'll read out. He says "transform" 5 times on camera, but his
email prompt (`EMAIL_COPY.md`) bans it in emails: his written rule wins.

## 11. The one-line test

**"Does this sound like Joel talking, or like a coach trying to sound like Joel?"**
Read it out loud at his pace. If a line wouldn't come out of his mouth between a "so" and a "right?", rewrite it.
